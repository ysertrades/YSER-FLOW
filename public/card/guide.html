Trades card editor

Principle

The preview canvas is the export canvas

Every other guarantee in this tool follows from that one decision.

Most editors keep a fast preview and a separate render path for the final file. The two drift — a label lands a pixel left, a shadow renders heavier, and the download never quite matches what you framed.

Here the on-screen canvas is declared at 1920 × 1080 and the export reads that same composition back. There is no second render path that can disagree with the first, so the downloaded PNG is the thing you looked at.

Good to know

The composition is always 1920 × 1080 — a fixed 16:9 frame. Export quality changes how many pixels that composition is rasterised into, never how it is laid out. A card framed at 1080p and a card framed at 8K are the same card.

  1. Screenshot inFile picker, drag and drop, or paste. Never leaves the tab.
  2. Working copyCapped at 3840 px on the long edge so panning stays smooth. The untouched original is kept alongside it.
  3. FrameZoom, offset and per-side trim are stored as numbers, not baked into pixels.
  4. OverlayTabs, badge, pill and wordmark are drawn as vectors and text — never as bitmaps.
  5. PNG outOverlays redrawn at the output resolution; image pixels pulled from the original, not the working copy.
Privacy

No server component. Nothing is stored, no cookies are set, and no data is sent anywhere. The only network request the page makes is for its two webfonts.

01Workflow

Load the screenshot

Three ways in. All of them land in the same place.

Load screenshot

Opens the file picker. Accepts anything the browser can decode as an image — PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, AVIF.

Drag and drop

Drop a file anywhere on the canvas area.

Paste

Press Ctrl / ⌘ V anywhere on the page. The fastest route: snip a chart, paste, export — no file ever hits your disk.

What happens the moment it lands

The original image is retained at full resolution. If its long edge exceeds 3840 px, a downscaled copy is built and the editor drives off that copy instead — which is why dragging a 6000 px screenshot around still feels immediate.

Framing state resets: zoom to 100%, offset to centre, all four trims to zero, fit mode to Fit.

Good to know

Using a lighter copy costs you nothing at export. When you export, the image pixels are read back out of the original, so no detail from the source screenshot is thrown away — the working copy exists only to keep the drag smooth.

HEIC will not load

Browsers cannot decode Apple's HEIC format. On iPhone, either take a screenshot — those are always PNG — or switch the camera over in Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.

02Workflow

Frame it

The part that takes the longest and matters the most.

Fit / Fill

Where framing starts. Fit shows the whole screenshot and pads the leftover edges with the card background. Fill covers the frame edge to edge and lets the overflow crop away. Either one resets zoom and position.

Zoom

Drag the slider (20–400%), or step with ±0.1% and ±0.5% for the last bit of alignment. Scroll or pinch on the canvas zooms toward the pointer and reaches 600%.

Position

Drag the canvas, nudge 1 px or 10 px with the buttons, or use the arrow keys — Shift makes each press 10 px. Centre returns the offset to 0, 0 without touching zoom.

Good to know

Hold Shift while scrolling and the zoom step drops from 2% to 0.2% per notch. It is the difference between hunting for an alignment and landing on it.

Trim edges

The control that solves half candles. Zooming to hide a half-drawn candle at the right edge moves everything else too. Trim works differently: it crops in source pixels, per side, before the frame is computed — so the edge goes away and the composition stays put.

Controls

Each side takes −1, +1, +10, or a typed value. Opposite sides cannot meet — at least 20 px of image always survives.

03Workflow

Set the labels

Symbol, session, timeframe. Everything else on the card is furniture.

Symbol

Seven instruments, each with a badge drawn from scratch on the canvas — no image files, which is why they stay crisp at 8K. The index futures carry the index size rather than the ticker, because that is what people recognise.

Metals

GC takes the gold mark, SI the silver one — the same three-bar glyph on a different ground.

Forex

Two overlapping flag discs, quote currency behind the base: EURUSD and GBPUSD.

Session

Three sessions — London, New York, Asia. Whichever is active is always pinned to the centre of the card; the other two rotate around it and fade outward, so the eye lands on the one that matters.

At 0 the two inactive labels sit flat grey. Raising it dissolves each one toward its outer end, away from the centre.

Timeframe

Ten presets, or type your own — up to six characters, uppercased as you type. Whatever you set lands in the pill above the rail, and in the exported filename.

04Workflow

Choose the ground

Change the background and the whole card follows it.

Six presets, a colour picker and a hex field. The default #F5F5F5 is untouched — but pick anything else and every part of the overlay re-derives itself rather than leaving dark type stranded on a dark card.

Glass

Drawn from the background, so it keeps the same relationship to it at every colour.

Type

Keeps a trace of the background's hue instead of snapping to pure black or white, and flips to light once the ground is dark. Every preset clears 14:1 contrast.

Session pill & edges

The pill inverts to light-on-dark grounds; hairlines and shadows swap with it, which is what keeps a panel legible once the card goes black.

Tone has always moved the panels 10 toward white and up to 32 toward grey. That asymmetry was never arbitrary — 10 is exactly the headroom #F5F5F5 has before it clips at white. So the range is now read off whatever ground you pick, and the default still lands on 10 and 32.

Good to know

Pure white is the one ground with no room above it. Rather than let the panels dissolve into the card, the separation is taken from the other side — the glass stays visible at every colour.

05Workflow

Tune the overlay

Two sliders decide whether the furniture sits on the chart or in it.

Overlay tone

The glass panels are drawn against a #f5f5f5 card background. Tone moves them relative to that background — and it is the control most worth learning, because a bright chart and a dark chart want opposite answers.

Overlay size

Scales every piece of furniture together, 80–120%. Tabs, badge, capsule, pill and wordmark keep their proportions to each other — one number, no drift.

Wordmark

The centred logo and handle, on by default. Pick the logo, type your own username, or switch it off for a clean card — all from the Wordmark panel. See the next slide.

Good to know

Tone changes fill and opacity together, and each element carries its own weight — the symbol capsule always reads slightly stronger than the rail behind it, the timeframe pill slightly stronger than both. Move the slider and that relationship survives; you cannot knock the three out of balance.

06Workflow

Sign the card

Your logo, your handle — or neither.

Logo

Two marks: X and Instagram. Both are drawn as vectors at export time, so neither softens at 8K.

Username

Type anything up to 20 characters. It is not forced to uppercase — unlike the timeframe field — so lowercase Instagram handles like yser.trades stay lowercase.

Show on card

Hides the whole wordmark. The rail, badge and pill are untouched.

Good to know

Clear the username entirely and the logo centres on its own, rather than leaving a dangling slash. A bare mark is often the cleaner signature.

Logo and username persist when you load the next screenshot, so a run of cards only needs this set once.

Solid X, outlined Instagram

The X mark is a filled glyph. The Instagram mark is deliberately not — it is stroked, so the chart behind it shows through the squircle and the lens instead of being punched out by a solid block.

Both are rendered in the card's ink, not brand colour, so the signature stays part of the card rather than competing with it.

07Workflow

Export

Lossless PNG. Four sizes. Three ways to get the file out.

1080p1920 × 1080Rendered at 2× and resolved down
4K3840 × 2160Default
6K5760 × 3240Native redraw
8K7680 × 4320Native redraw

Why the big sizes are actually bigger

Nothing is upscaled. Every label, badge and panel is drawn again at the output size rather than stretched from the preview, and the image pixels come from your original screenshot, not the editing copy.

Each size is also rendered larger than it is saved and resolved down, which smooths the curve of a badge and the edge of the type — a full double pass at 1080p and 4K, a partial one at 6K.

Your screenshot is the ceiling

That cleans up the overlay, not the chart. A 1080p screenshot exported at 8K gives crisp badges over a chart with no more detail than it started with. Grab the source large and the big sizes pay off.

Filenames

Exports name themselves from what is on the card: NQ-NewYork-H4-3840x2160.png. Sort a folder of them and they group by instrument on their own.

Getting the file out

A download fires automatically, then a bar appears with a thumbnail and three routes. This is deliberate: inside an embedded frame a plain download can be blocked by the host's sandbox, and at least one of these always works.

Download

Fires the download again, by hand.

Share

The native share sheet. Appears only where the device supports sharing files — mostly phones.

Open in tab

Opens the PNG on its own so you can save it manually. The universal fallback.

If a device cannot allocate a canvas that large, the export steps down and tells you to pick a smaller size rather than failing silently.

Control reference — the card

Everything that becomes part of the composition.

Symbol

Futures NQ ES YM GC SI, forex EURUSD GBPUSD. Default NQ.

Session

London New York Asia. The active one is centred and the other two rotate around it. Default New York.

Fade

0–100. How far the two inactive session labels dissolve toward their outer ends. 0 leaves them flat grey. Default 100.

Timeframe

M1 M3 M5 M15 M30 H1 H4 H6 D1 W1, or a custom label up to 6 characters, forced uppercase. Default H4.

Background

Six presets, a colour picker, or a typed hex. Every overlay colour derives from it. Default #F5F5F5; Reset returns to it.

Overlay size

80–120%. Scales all furniture together. Default 100%.

Overlay tone

−100 to +100. flat at zero melts the panels into the background; positive lifts them toward white; negative sinks them into a soft grey. Default +50.

Wordmark — logo

X filled, or Instagram as an outline the chart shows through. Default X.

Wordmark — username

Free text up to 20 characters, case preserved. Empty centres the logo on its own. Default YSERTRADES.

Show on card

Hides the centred logo and handle without touching the rail, badge or pill. On by default.

ReferenceFraming & output

Control reference — the frame

Everything that decides which pixels land inside the composition.

Zoom

Slider 20–400%. Scroll and pinch reach 600%. Steps of ±0.1% and ±0.5%; scroll steps 2%, or 0.2% with Shift. Zooming by gesture keeps the point under your cursor fixed.

Fit / Fill

Fit contains the whole screenshot, Fill covers the frame. Both reset zoom to 100% and position to centre. Default Fit.

Position

Offset in card pixels from centre. Drag, arrow keys, or ±1 and ±10 buttons. Centre zeroes it without changing zoom.

Trim edges

Per-side crop measured in source pixels, applied before framing. Opposite sides are prevented from meeting — 20 px of image always remains.

Export quality

1080p 4K 6K 8K, all lossless PNG. Default 4K.

Reset

Returns zoom, position, all four trims and fit mode to defaults. Keeps your screenshot and your labels.

Remove screenshot

Clears the image and all framing, and frees the memory it was holding. Labels and overlay settings survive.

The sixty-second card

Once it is muscle memory, this is the whole loop.

  1. Snip the chartWhatever your OS uses. Do not save it — leave it on the clipboard.
  2. PasteCtrl / ⌘ V anywhere on the page.
  3. Fill, then trimHit Fill, then trim the edge with the half candle. Two clicks, no drift.
  4. Set the labelsSymbol, session, timeframe — and your logo and handle, once. All of it persists between screenshots.
  5. Check the toneBright chart, push tone negative. Dark chart, leave it lifted.
  6. Export4K is the default and the right answer almost always.
Remember

What you see in the preview is the file. If the card looks right on screen, it is right in the download — there is no second render that can disagree.
